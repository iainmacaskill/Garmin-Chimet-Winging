Wing Foil Performance Analysis

Which Solent spot, and why

A methodology for picking between Meon / Hill Head, Emsworth, Hayling Island, West Wittering, Hurst Castle / Milford, Calshot Spit and Lepe, built from live wind, tide and swell data.

The short version

With five wing spots along a 20‑mile stretch of coast, the wind speed alone rarely picks a winner — on most SW–S days two or three of them are all "on." The variable that actually discriminates is wind direction relative to each spot's coastline, with tide as a secondary factor at the two spots that dry out over sandbanks (Hayling and West Wittering).

Rule of thumb: pick the spot whose coastline the wind is blowing across, not off or straight into a headland. A 15 kn wind can be the best session of the month at one spot and unrideable eight miles away, for no reason other than the angle it arrives at.

Spot-by-spot geography

Meon / Hill Head wingE – S – WSW

Faces roughly south across the Solent with open fetch to the Isle of Wight. Cross-shore from the east through south to WSW; the wind stays clean because there's deep water close to the launch. N–NE is offshore here and should be treated as a no‑go regardless of speed. One of the most tide-tolerant spots in the group — rideable across most of the tidal range.

Emsworth wingSE – SW

Tucked into the top of Chichester Harbour, so it needs more south in the wind to stay onshore, and shallows out hard away from high water. Best sailed within a couple of hours of HW; northerlies are offshore and gusty off the town moorings.

Hayling Island wingESE – W

Open seafront with a wide sector — takes anything from ESE around to W cleanly. Big tidal range over sand; needs mid‑to‑high tide for clean water, since low tide exposes banks well out from the beach. North is offshore.

West Wittering wingSE – W

The widest good sector of the group — holds cross-shore wind further round towards WNW than any other spot, which is why it's usually the pick on a westerly-veering day. Same sandbank tide-dependency as Hayling; NE is offshore.

Hurst Castle / Milford wingS – W

Sits right at the Needles Channel, which funnels a southwesterly and often makes this the first spot to go off and the strongest on a SW day, even when the central Solent spots are still marginal. Trade-off: a strong ebb running against a SW'ly kicks up serious short-period chop through the Narrows. N–NE is offshore over the spit.

Calshot Spit wingS – W

Sits right at the mouth of Southampton Water, where the shingle spit gives two very different sessions on the same wind: sheltered, flat water tucked behind the bar at low‑to‑mid tide, opening out to full Solent swell as the tide comes up. Best on S–W; N–NE is offshore across the water towards Southampton.

Lepe wingSE – W

Open north-shore frontage on the western Solent between Calshot and Hurst, looking across to Cowes and the Beaulieu River entrance. No live meter sits at Lepe itself — the dashboard reads the nearest confirmed live stations at Hurst Castle and Calshot as a bracket, since a SW'ly reliably fills in across this whole stretch of coast together. N–NE is offshore.

Decision guide by wind direction

This is the logic the live dashboard applies automatically — read as "if the wind is coming from roughly here, these spots are on."

N – NEOffshore at every spot. No‑go regardless of speed; if you're set on riding, look for Meon in a sheltered pocket only in light airs.
E – ESEMeon's angle — clean cross-shore. Hayling and Wittering start to work as it backs further south.
SEHayling, Wittering, Lepe and Emsworth all switch on. Meon still good.
SDead onshore at Hayling and Wittering — straightforward but can be choppy close in. Meon, Emsworth, Calshot and Lepe all solid.
SSW – SWEverywhere works. Hurst Castle is usually strongest here due to the Needles Channel funnel; Calshot and Wittering are close, cleaner options.
WSW – WWittering holds the longest of the group (good sector runs to 280°); Calshot, Hurst and Lepe still on. Meon and Hayling start going side-off and gustier.
WNW – NWMarginal everywhere; only Wittering is still plausibly on, and only right at the top of its sector.

Scoring thresholds

The live dashboard turns the rules above into a GO / MARGINAL / NO‑GO call per spot, using:

BandWind speedVerdict
Too light< 12 knNo‑go
Marginal12–15 knMarginal (big wing)
Good15–28 knGo
Strong28–35 knGo (small wing)
Too much> 35 knNo‑go

A gust spread over 12 kn above the mean, or a direction outside the spot's good sector, downgrades a Go to Marginal. These thresholds and the direction sectors above are config values in the dashboard, not hard-coded — they get tuned as real sessions confirm or contradict them.

Worked example — Wittering vs Hayling vs Meon. On a typical SW day, all three read broadly similar wind speed. West Wittering usually wins on angle alone: SW arrives closer to side-on with the longest open fetch of the three. Hayling is a close second on the same wind. Meon takes the win instead when the wind sits further round in the SE–E quadrant, where it's clean cross-shore and Wittering/Hayling are more end-on. Tide breaks remaining ties at Wittering and Hayling — favour the one closer to mid/high water when speed and angle are otherwise level.
